# BLACKOUT DevLog

A running record of what the team built, what went wrong and what we learned. Dates are 2026.

## Week of 2 Nov: Concept and plan
We wanted something that works in VR for a global audience and that we could finish in two weeks. Early ideas were too basic. We settled on **BLACKOUT**: a city-wide power cut, a pitch-dark night market, sonar pings and 3D sound to find your way, and a gradual relight as the reward. Darkness also made the art cheap to produce because the player only ever sees what the sonar reveals, and a dark scene is easier to run on Quest 2.

We split the work into three independent tracks so nobody waited on anybody: Experience and Accessibility, Sonar and Gameplay, World and Audio. Each track owns its folder and talks to the others through two small contracts: `GameFlow.OnDelivered()` and `CaptionSystem.Instance.Show(id)`.

Safety decision on day one: no flashing, cooldown above one second, slow relight, and a "reduce flashing" option that is on by default.

## 7 Nov: Project setup
Unity 6.4 VR template, URP 17.4, XR Interaction Toolkit 3.4.1, OpenXR with Meta Quest and Touch controller profiles. Android target: ARM64, IL2CPP, Vulkan. Assembly definitions for each track so scripts compile separately.

**Problem:** the disk was full and Unity could not import. **Fix:** cleared old projects; later cleared more when builds failed again.

## 8 Nov: Track 1, UI and accessibility
Built a world-space UI system with laser-pointer clicks and a gaze-dwell fallback, a caption system fed by a CSV, settings that persist, a state machine for the game flow, and ten automated tests.

**Problem:** quick trigger taps did not register on buttons in the editor. **Cause:** press and release landed in the same frame and the Input System UI ignored it. **Fix:** held presses in testing, and made the button visuals independent of click timing.

**Decision:** captions follow the head gently and use a minimum 36 pt text size so they stay readable on a Quest 2 lens.

## 8 Nov: Kenney assets and sound
Imported seven Kenney CC0 packs and wired Kenney interface sounds into the menu (hover, click, back, confirm). Built the first Quest APK (68 MB).

## 9 Nov: Track 3, the city
Wrote a scene builder that generates a 29 by 29 tile city (about 350 m across): a road grid, a skyscraper core, commercial blocks, an industrial district, a market with 75 stalls, street lamps, neon signs, props and a 70 m radio tower.

**Performance plan for Quest 2:** a handful of shared materials, no real-time shadows, static batching, and a pool of eight real-time lights that follow the player while every lamp shows a glowing bulb. Per-pixel lights are limited to 3 per object and the shadow map was cut from 4096 to 2048.

**Problem:** the first lit render looked flat and the models' facing was a guess. **Fix:** adjusted material smoothness, switched palette textures to point filtering and checked road and building directions in the editor.

**Relight:** a smooth power change over 3.5 seconds (5 with Reduce Flashing). Fog, ambient, moonlight, lamps and neon signs all ease up together.

## 10 Nov: Track 2, sonar and gameplay
Wrote the sonar: 96 azimuths by 11 elevations of rays cast at the moment of the ping, with each echo appearing when the expanding wave reaches it, which makes the sound and the picture feel connected. Echoes are particles so the whole effect is one draw call. A ring spreads along the ground and a soft light pulse follows.

Objective: three relays, each with its own looping tone. Only the current relay plays, so players follow sound. A relay switches on when the player pings near it or stands still for 3.5 seconds, so players who cannot ping still progress. After the third relay the tower signal starts. Captions give directions in words every 30 seconds if the player stalls.

**Audio:** all sounds were generated by a Python script (sine sweeps, filtered noise, pulse trains), so there are no licence issues and the sounds fit the mood: a 55 Hz generator hum, market murmur, distant traffic with danfo horns, relay tones with echo, and a tower signal of three rising beeps.

**Problem:** the first echoes were square and sparse. **Fix:** a procedural soft dot texture and more rays.

## 10 Nov: Disk space again
The C: drive fell to 241 MB and Unity warned. We deleted two old course archives and kept only what the build needs.

## 11 Nov: Monsters and people
Added roaming creatures from the Asset Store (Orc and Skeleton) and idle townspeople in the market. Monsters patrol the road grid (no NavMesh needed), notice the player by sight or sound, charge, and die at dawn. Sonar pings are loud, so the main tool is also the main risk. Difficulty: Normal, Gentle (outwalkable) or Off. Added a heartbeat, captions, glowing eyes and haptics as warnings, and a gentle "They found you" screen. 9 new tests cover difficulty, perception and road navigation (25 total). **Problem:** a monster standing at the player hid the end screen. **Fix:** the catching monster is stepped back before the screen shows.

## 12 Nov: One lantern, real puzzles

Playtest feedback from the headset: too much light everywhere and not enough to do. The city is now pitch black. Your only light is a handheld lantern (left hand). It burns oil, can be covered with the grip button to hide from monsters, and is refilled by oil flasks found on the streets. A pale guide spirit drifts toward the goal for a few seconds now and then. The route is now: three relays, a brass key on a pedestal ringed with pits, three plaque clues (one beside each relay) giving a random brazier order, three braziers to light in that order (wrong order puts them out and makes noise), then the sealed door at the tower. Traps: pits (fade to black and return to the last relay), tripwires (a bell that calls monsters). Hiding places (crate alcoves) make monsters lose you if you cover the lantern and stay still. Monsters now see a covered lantern much less. Sonar cooldown raised to 3 s. Beat the dawn is 10 minutes. All 25 tests still pass.

## What we learned
- Sound-first design makes a small art budget feel large
- A static scene builder (code that builds the scene) saved hours and keeps the scene reproducible
- Accessibility is easier when the rule is set on day one and a test enforces it
- Test on the headset early; the editor hides comfort and performance problems
